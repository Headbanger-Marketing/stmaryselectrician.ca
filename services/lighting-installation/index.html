Plan the light and the work that makes it possible

A lighting project may involve fixture replacement, new wiring points or a different control layout. The contractor needs to assess existing wiring, mounting support, equipment suitability and access. Fixture appearance alone does not establish compatibility with a dimmer or the suitability of a location. Share product information and the intended room use before installation is priced.

Prepare the scope for your St. Marys property

Sketch the room in words, with the door, the work areas and where light is needed most. Photograph the existing fixtures and switches, and gather instructions for any fixtures you have already bought.

Ask whether the quote covers new switch positions, fixtures suited to a damp or dusty space and the repair of any ceiling or wall opened. Ask what is assumed about the existing wiring where records are missing.

What should the assessment establish?

Where does a St. Marys workshop or garage get power for its new lights, and does that wiring suit the space? The report traces the source and gives the contractor's judgement on its suitability. Which points are new, which are reused, and which switches control them? Each point is marked new or reused, and every fixture is named beside its control, with the surfaces to be restored. What could not be traced? In an older building with past alterations, those gaps are listed with how they affect the proposal. Quotes built that way can be compared fixture for fixture, not by totals.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Are fixtures meant for living rooms fine in a garage or shop?

Not always, especially if the space is damp, dusty or unheated. Ask the contractor to confirm that each fixture is suited to the location, and share the product sheets so the quote reflects what you actually plan to buy.

Can new lights share wiring that was added to the building years ago?

That is the contractor's call after looking at the wiring, not something to assume from the room. Each quote needs to state whether existing wiring is reused or replaced, and what happens if it proves unsuitable.
