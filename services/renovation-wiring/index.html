Renovation wiring in St. Marys.

Moving a laundry or mechanical area in a St. Marys home shifts the equipment that needs power, and often the wiring that fed it. A clear scope shows what moves, what stays and what is made good.

Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your St. Marys property

Mark the current and the planned position of each appliance on a simple sketch. Collect the appliance specification sheets and photograph any visible cables, labels or patched walls near the old location.

Ask what happens to the old wiring and receptacles once the equipment moves, and who is responsible for making the walls good. Ask the contractor to say in writing where concealed wiring is expected and how surprises are handled.

What should the assessment establish?

The laundry or mechanical area of a St. Marys home is moving. What the new location needs is weighed against what the old one supplied, and the contractor says whether each existing circuit is extended, replaced or retired. Any unlabelled or spliced wiring left by past alterations is recorded, with a note of how it was dealt with. The scope states the ESA notification, any building permission and who restores the opened surfaces. Where two proposals treat the old circuits differently, find out from each contractor how the wiring left behind will be made safe and labelled.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

If the washer and dryer relocate, is the wiring replaced?

Not always, but the contractor must assess whether the existing circuit can be extended or needs replacing. Ask each quote to say which choice it assumes and why, because that assumption often explains the difference between proposals.

Who restores the wall after the old laundry connections are removed?

It depends on what each quote includes, and some leave finishing to another trade. Check whether patching, painting and any flooring repair are covered or excluded, so you are not comparing unlike scopes.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
