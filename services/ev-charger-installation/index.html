EV charger installation in St. Marys.

Two vehicles sharing one supply in a St. Marys home raises a different question from a single charger. The assessment has to consider both vehicles and everything else running at the same time.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your St. Marys property

Write down where each vehicle parks, how far that spot is from the panel and the way a cable could reach it. Collect the charger product sheets and any earlier wiring paperwork you can find.

Ask whether the quote installs two chargers or one shared arrangement, and how the design behaves when both vehicles plug in together. Ask which parts are priced separately if existing wiring needs more work.

What should the assessment establish?

Agreeing to one charging proposal for two vehicles depends on the assessment showing how the planned charging fits with the loads a St. Marys home already carries. Each quote therefore states whether it proposes a load-management arrangement, a second dedicated circuit or a change to the supply. Where earlier owners added circuits without paperwork, the quote explains how those unrecorded loads were counted. It also names the charging equipment, where it mounts and whether outdoor-rated equipment is required. When one quote proposes load management and another a supply change, it is worth asking each contractor why the other approach was not chosen.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is one supply able to serve two vehicles charging together?

It depends on the supply and the design, which the contractor must assess. Ask for the proposed arrangement in writing, including what the system does when both vehicles are plugged in at once.

How should I compare a one-charger quote with a two-charger quote?

Ask each contractor to state the assessed load, the circuits added, the equipment included and what is left for later. A quote that looks smaller may simply assume less, so compare what each one covers.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Vehicle and charger model if chosen
  • Parking position and preferred equipment location
  • Indoor or outdoor conditions
  • Existing supply records if available
  • Other major loads and future charging needs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
