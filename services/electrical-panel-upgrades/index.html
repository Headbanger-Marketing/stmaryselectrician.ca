Panel and service upgrades in St. Marys.

A St. Marys panel or service upgrade calls for a close read of each quote. Separating the supply for a second living unit, for example, means each contractor should explain how every unit is fed, metered and shut off.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your St. Marys property

List who lives in or uses each unit, where the existing meter and panel sit, and which rooms are shared. Take photos of the closed panel and the labels on its cover without opening anything, and note any additions made over the years.

Ask each contractor whether the quote includes the utility steps, the ESA notification and any wall or ceiling opening needed to separate circuits. Ask what happens if earlier alterations turn out to be undocumented.

What should the assessment establish?

Where a home in St. Marys has a second living unit, how the two supplies are kept apart and which equipment each unit controls are for the contractor to explain. The written assessment covers whether the existing service can feed both or whether the utility must change the incoming supply. In an older house, the contractor notes any circuit crossing between the units because of past alterations. The scope lists what is replaced, what stays and which surfaces are restored. The homeowner compares quotes on that common footing. The utility contact and the ESA notification belong to whichever party each quote names.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

When a second unit is added, what separates its supply from the main one?

That is for the contractor and the utility to assess, not a guess from the layout. Ask each quote to state whether it creates a separate panel, a separate supply or both, and what the unit can switch off independently.

Why do two quotes for splitting the supply differ in scope?

One may include utility contact, wall repairs and the notification while another leaves them out. Line the inclusions up in a short list, then ask each contractor to confirm in writing what is excluded.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Reason for the upgrade and known concerns
  • Existing major loads and proposed additions
  • Panel information visible without removing covers
  • Equipment location and access restrictions
  • Preferred timing and equipment that must remain operating

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
